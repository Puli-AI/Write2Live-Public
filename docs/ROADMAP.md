# Product roadmap

Write2Live 1.0 is the controlled-access launch, not the end of development. The aim is to help people become more capable writers rather than simply generate polished text for them. The broader service-family vision is described in [Platform direction](PLATFORM-DIRECTION.md).

## Direction

The product loop is:

1. choose or discover a meaningful direction;
2. write under chosen time and momentum constraints;
3. request feedback after composing;
4. practise a specific next skill; and
5. see progress across attempts.

## Product-family direction

Write2Live is planned as the common learning core for:

- academic research and writing support for MBA, EMBA, DBA, PhD, undergraduate, and postgraduate learners;
- syllabus-aligned English learning, beginning with HKDSE and designed for later regional syllabus packs; and
- targeted writing games for different ages and learner profiles.

The learner remains the author. AI can support discovery, planning, questioning, feedback, revision, and reflection, while shared consent, protected drafting, learner-owned history, data controls, progress, and evaluation standards remain part of the core.

## Planned releases

| Release | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| v1.0 | Controlled access, secure writer accounts, focused sessions, optional AI feedback, progress, and administration. |
| v1.1 | Writer-owned completed-session review, self-service data export/deletion, retention automation, restore rehearsal, monitoring, accessibility, and administrator controls. |
| v1.2 | Versioned rubrics, educator-marked benchmarks, score stability testing, drift checks, and clearer feedback interpretation. |
| v1.3 | Small writing games for fluency, concision, register, evidence, cohesion, and endings. |
| v1.4 | Guided learning paths using repeated practice and demonstrated needs. |
| v1.5 | Consent-aware educator assignments, cohorts, and review tools. |
| v2.0 | An educator-reviewed HKDSE-aligned practice suite planned with Hong Kong Beacon. |
| Post-v2.0 | An applied-research pilot for MBA, EMBA, and DBA learners, using controlled research integrations and evidence-to-claim traceability. |
| Later | Broader academic levels and additional regional syllabus packs, each subject to partner, benchmark, safety, integrity, and viability gates. |

The sequence may change when evidence or partner capacity changes. Dates will be set only when the work and validation inputs are known.

The intended order is to complete core trust and feedback evidence, make HKDSE the first focused vertical and first home for three writing-game prototypes, then pilot MBA/EMBA/DBA applied-research workflows. PhD and new regional syllabus support come later, after the necessary integrity, disciplinary, local-partner, and benchmark safeguards are in place.

## Writer-owned session review

A future progress row should let the signed-in writer reopen one completed session and see the original input, refined text, criterion scores, strengths, areas for growth, suggestions, exercises, and next focus together.

This view should be owner-only by default. Administrator or educator access to student writing requires a separate, explicit consent model, scoped roles, audit history, and retention rules; it must not be inferred from operational administrator access.

## Academic research and writing direction

The planned academic suite will guide learners through research framing, evidence use, argument development, staged writing, revision, provenance review, and supervisor-ready checkpoints. Early pilots should focus on MBA, EMBA, and DBA applied research. Selected capabilities from Puli StrategyLab may be used through controlled integrations, while the two products and their data remain separate.

The service is intended to support learning and responsible research, not ghostwrite assessed work. Broader undergraduate and postgraduate uses can follow successful pilots. PhD support requires later, stronger disciplinary, citation, supervisor, and research-integrity validation.

## HKDSE direction

Puli Consulting plans to work with Hong Kong Beacon to explore both short writing games and structured student practice based on published curriculum outcomes, task types, exemplars, and marking guidance.

Planned principles:

- practise context, audience, purpose, register, organization, and language use;
- support drafting and revision rather than only one-shot scoring;
- use versioned, educator-reviewed tasks and rubrics;
- compare AI feedback with independently marked student work;
- protect student privacy and use age-appropriate notices; and
- avoid implying HKEAA endorsement or predicted HKDSE levels without validation.

Writing games should target one observable skill, preserve the learner's work, include reflection or retry, and test transfer to a later authentic task. After validation in HKDSE work, suitable games may be adapted for other ages, profiles, and syllabuses.

Official references include the [EDB English Language Curriculum and Assessment Guide](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/eng-edu/Curriculum%20Document/English%20Language%20Curriculum%20and%20Assessment%20Guide%20%28Secondary%204%20-%206%29%20%282021%29.pdf) and [HKEAA HKDSE subject information and sample papers](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/en/hkdse/assessment/subject_information/category_a_subjects/hkdse_subj.html).

## Commercial direction

Write2Live remains controlled access while the team measures repeat use, learning value, provider cost, and support needs. Future options include learner session packs, subscriptions, school/cohort licences, and sponsored access. Any paid offer should use clear professional terms and transparent data, cancellation, and support policies.
